# Trademark policy

**cig-orchestrator**, **cigo** and the cig-orchestrator logo are trademarks of
Jay Taylor, as **CigScript** is. The code is under FSL-1.1-ALv2, and that license says so itself in its
Trademarks section: it grants no trademark rights. This page says
what that means in practice, so nobody has to guess.

The short version: use the names to say what your thing is made of or works
with; do not use them to say what your thing *is*.

## Free to do, no permission needed

- Say that a project **uses cigo**, **is orchestrated with cig-orchestrator**,
  **ships a `cig-tasks.json`** or **is compatible with cigo**, when that is
  accurate.
- Write toolchains, manifests, examples, editor plugins and teaching material
  for cigo. The manifest format, the task keys, the stage names and the
  knowledge base's shape are not claimed as trademarks or as protected
  expression.
- Use the names in articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, reviews and
  criticism, including unflattering ones.
- Distribute unmodified official releases under their names, with the LICENSE
  and NOTICE files intact, as the license requires.
- Name a package, plugin or repository in a way that describes its
  relationship truthfully, such as `cigo-toolchains-extra`, `cigo-vscode` or
  `awesome-cigo`, provided it is clear the project is not the official one.

## Ask first

- Naming a **fork, product, service, company or domain** "cig-orchestrator",
  "cigo" or a confusingly similar name (Cigo, CigOrchestrator, cig-orch,
  cigo.dev). Forks are possible under the license's terms; they need their own name once
  they differ from the official releases.
- Using the **logo** anywhere other than to link to the official project.
- Calling a build, distribution or service **official**, **certified**, or
  otherwise implying endorsement.
- Merchandise carrying the names or the logo.

Use GitHub's private reporting on this repository (see `SECURITY.md`) or open
a discussion with what you have in mind. Answers are usually quick, and the
usual answer to a truthful, non-confusing use is yes.
